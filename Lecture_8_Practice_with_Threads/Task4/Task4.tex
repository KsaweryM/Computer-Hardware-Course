\settitle{Task 4}{Robots in a warehouse}

\begin{story}
A company runs an automatic warehouse: robots drive around a grid of cells. The company says that its robots
can never get stuck. Prove them wrong: write routes after which the robots are stuck for ever.
\end{story}

Every robot is a thread, and every cell of the grid is a mutex. A robot \textbf{holds} the lock of the cell it
stands on. One step takes four moments:

\begin{center}

So a robot never stands on no cell, and two robots never stand on one cell.

The program is \textbf{ready}: you do not change the code, only the input. The routes are in the file
\texttt{routes.txt}. The grid has 5 rows (0 to 4) and 8 columns (0 to 7); the cell (0,0) is in the top left corner.

\codefile[style=plain]{routes.txt}

\runline

A route is a list of steps: \texttt{R} right, \texttt{L} left, \texttt{U} up, \texttt{D} down. The last number is
the time of one step of this robot in milliseconds.
When a robot has finished its route, it leaves the warehouse: it unlocks its cell and disappears from the grid.

After every step the program prints where the robot went and draws the grid:

\begin{terminal}
$ ./build/robots                # reads routes.txt
start:
  A . . . . . . .
  . . . . . . . .
  . . . . . . . .
  . . . . . . . .
  . . . . . . . B

robot A: (0,0) -> (0,1)
  . A . . . . . .
  . . . . . . . .
  . . . . . . . .
  . . . . . . . .
  . . . . . . . B

...
$ ./build/robots other.txt      # reads other.txt
\end{terminal}

When every robot that has not finished has waited for more than 2\,s, the program prints who waits for whom
and stops:

\begin{terminal}
DEADLOCK: nobody can move.
  robot A waits for (2,4), which robot B holds
  robot B waits for (2,3), which robot A holds
\end{terminal}


\section{Your tasks}

\begin{questions}
  \item Write routes for 2 robots after which they are stuck for ever (a deadlock).
  \item Does your deadlock happen every time, or only sometimes? Change the speeds of the robots and check.
  \item Three robots drive in a line, like a snake: A right behind B, B right behind C, all on the same path.
        Each one may have a different speed, so a faster robot catches up with the slower one in front and waits
        for its cell. Can they get stuck for ever? Why? Write such routes and check.

\end{questions}
